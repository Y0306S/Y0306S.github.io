Automated dubbing pipeline

iQIYI International · full stack intern · Aug 2026 – Jan 2027

The problem

Dubbing an episode by hand means cutting audio, matching mouths, and stitching speech that still sounds like it was assembled. I worked on an end-to-end stack that takes an episode from audio prep through model inference to segment synthesis, so the dubbed speech comes out as one coherent pass.

What I did

  • Wired preprocessing, inference, and synthesis into one pipeline instead of a pile of manual steps.
  • Compared and fine-tuned TTS and voice-conversion models (GPT-SoVITS, Qwen-3.0-TTS, Seed-VC, Chatterbox) for cross-lingual inference.
  • Fine-tuned speech emotion recognition (emotion2vec, wav2vec2) so background characters can borrow emotion- and timbre-matched reference audio.
  • Frontend and backend on the dubbing site: JavaScript, CSS, Python (Flask, Celery).

What came out

About 67% faster than the manual baseline — roughly 7–10 hours saved per episode.
